4The node, part 1: the body

Theory Guide §5 derived rn = √((L/2n)² + (W/2)²). Here it is as code. Nothing is hand-typed: the radii come from the formula, so changing the plank size changes every disc correctly.

disc_chain(3) cuts the plank into three slices, 0.167 m long each, and puts a coin on each slice big enough to cover its corners. offsets says where the coins sit along the robot's nose-to-tail line. radius says how big they are.

SAFETY_MARGIN = 0.02 m is added to every keep-out (it's also a parameter, safety_margin). It wasn't in the first version. It was added after the first s1a run, where the three-disc plank grazed the obstacle by 2 mm because odometry was 2.8 cm off the truth (§13, §14). With it, the gap each footprint needs becomes 2(r + 0.02): 0.579 m for one disc and 0.300 m for three.

sweep is the same for one and three because it describes the real plank, and it is only used to shrink the box. So the box treats both footprints identically and can't confound the comparison.

rectangle_clearance is Theory Guide §15's clamp formula. The controller never calls it. It sits here so the plank's size is written down in exactly one place, and the desk sim and analysis both import it.

8The node, part 5: what it draws

marker() fills in the four fields that fail silently (Lab 2 report): a unique id, orientation.w = 1, alpha above 0, and scale as a diameter. publish_footprint draws the plank as an orange box and the discs as blue flat cylinders at the measured pose. 9The node, part 6: starting and stopping

{{code|mpc_node.py| def stop|if __name__}}
Lab 2's main() called plain rclpy.init(). In Jazzy, Ctrl-C then makes rclpy's own signal handler shut the context down first, and the finally: node.stop() publish fails with "publisher's context is invalid". The robot is never told to stop. On a real TurtleBot it keeps its last command until the motor watchdog, if there is one. Found in the first headless run, where the node log ended in that exact traceback. SignalHandlerOptions.NO means Ctrl-C arrives as a plain KeyboardInterrupt, stop() publishes zero, and then we shut down.

12The evaluation harness

{{code|plot_run.py|def grade|def report}}
  • Clearance uses ground truth and the rectangle, never odometry and never discs (Theory Guide §15).
  • Path error is the distance from the true position to the reference curve, not to the moving reference point. That needs no clock alignment between the bag and the node's start time, and it measures what the eye sees: how far the robot left the line.
  • Goal reached means within 0.08 m of ground truth. The node stops at 0.05 m by odometry, and odometry sits 1–2 cm off truth in Gazebo, so 0.05 by truth would mark some correct stops as failures.
  • Pass (scenario 2): goal reached and clearance > 0 to every obstacle.
  • Reached in is timed from the first non-zero /cmd_vel, so Gazebo and node start-up aren't counted (a code-review fix: before it, 8.9 s read as 12.7 s).

14Silent failures we hit

Gate 1 task 3: the goal that never arrived. Two bags, 671 and 1307 /cmd_vel messages, every one exactly zero, and 0 predictions. /new_position is not even a topic in either bag, so the recorder never saw a publisher. Either the goal was published before the node existed (a ros2 topic pub message isn't stored for late subscribers), or from a terminal on another domain. How it was proved: same node and command, goal published 10 s after the node printed its first line, and the robot drove to (1.78, 0.01). Lesson: the node logs NEW GOAL. No such line means no goal, and nothing else will tell you.
"RViz shows nothing of the robot." The Lab 2 config has no display that draws the body: no RobotModel, and no marker for the footprint. Odometry arrows and TF axes are easy to miss. Fixed with a RobotModel display and /mpc_footprint. Verified by capturing the RViz window in a headless run: everything shows, with or without use_sim_time, so the clock was not the cause. A terminal on another domain would also give an empty RViz, so always check printenv ROS_DOMAIN_ID first.
Solve time tripled by a window. The first desk sweep gave a 118 ms median for Lab 2's own task. Your Gazebo GUI was still open. With it closed: 20–33 ms. Any solve-time number must say whether the GUI was running.
Ctrl-C didn't stop the robot. §9. Inherited from Lab 2, and invisible unless you read the node's last log lines.
The graze nobody saw coming. First s1a run with three discs: the plan rode its keep-out with about 4 mm of real clearance to spare. At closest approach odometry was 2.8 cm off ground truth, so the real plank touched the obstacle (−2.1 mm). The contact made the wheels slip, odometry drifted 1.03 m from the truth, and the robot calmly tracked a circle in the wrong place for the rest of the lap. Every node log line looked fine. Only ground truth showed it. Lesson: a tighter footprint spends exactly the slack that used to absorb odometry error. Fix: a 2 cm safety margin, as Lab 3 did with its inflation radius.
Starting inside a keep-out. s1b's first obstacle position put the origin inside the one-disc keep-out. The soft constraint would have shoved the robot out, and the comparison would have measured that shove instead of the footprint. Caught by arithmetic before any run (LOG D2).

15Things to try yourself

Each one is a single change, and each has a prediction you can check. Use the desk sim first (seconds), then Gazebo.

Two discs. Add 'two': dict(offsets=disc_chain(2)[0], radius=disc_chain(2)[1], sweep=PLANK_SWEEP) to FOOTPRINTS. Prediction: r = 0.160, so with the margin it needs 2(0.16 + 0.02) = 0.36 m. It passes s2_040 and rejects s2_034.
python3 desk/sim_scenario.py --scenario s2_034 --footprint two
Make the slack cheap. -p obstacle_penalty:=10.0. Prediction: one disc starts squeezing through gaps it should reject, and real clearance drops. That shows why ρ must beat the tracking cost.
Longer horizon. -p n_horizon:=40 with gui:=false, and read /mpc_solve_time. Prediction: p95 near 170 ms on s2_030 three, over the tick. Watch whether the motion gets worse.
Turn delay compensation off. -p delay_steps:=0 on s1a. Lab 2 predicted more jerk with uncompensated delay. Compare the path error.
Take the margin away. -p safety_margin:=0.0. Prediction: three discs pass s2_028 again, and s1a three-disc grazes the obstacle again. The old bags in bags/margin0/ are exactly this experiment.

16Oral questions, with answers

Walk me through one control tick.Check odometry is fresh. Write the reference for each of the N+1 steps into the TVP. Push the measured pose forward by one tick using the last command (delay compensation). Call make_step, which solves the whole horizon with IPOPT. Time it and publish the time. If the solve failed, hold the last command for up to three ticks. Otherwise clip and publish the first input, and publish the predicted path.
How does the solver know where the plank is?It doesn't know about the plank. It knows three points, x + d·cos θ and y + d·sin θ for d ∈ {−0.167, 0, 0.167}, as CasADi expressions of the state, and it keeps each one at least ro + 0.130 from every obstacle centre.
How do you know the plank didn't hit anything?Gazebo's ground-truth pose, the exact 0.50 × 0.20 rectangle, and the clamp distance to each obstacle circle at every sample. The minimum over the run is reported. It's independent of both odometry and the disc model.
Why is the box soft? Isn't that unsafe?A hard bound on a measured state is unrecoverable once odometry puts you outside it: Lab 2 task 4. A linear penalty with ρ = 10⁴ is exact, so slack is zero whenever staying inside is possible. In scenario 2 the Gazebo walls are real, so any squeeze would show as contact in the ground-truth clearance.
Why N = 20 when you planned to measure 20, 40 and 60?We did measure. With the Gazebo window closed, N = 40 has a 95th-percentile solve of 174 ms on the hardest scenario, beyond the 100 ms tick. N = 20 has 59 ms. Its reach of 0.44 m is enough for scenarios 1 and 2, because every detour there is under 0.2 m.
What did three discs cost in computation?Read it from the solve-time column in §13: about 1.5× the median of one disc (33 against 22 ms), not 3×, because IPOPT's cost isn't proportional to the number of constraints.
What does the one-disc robot do at a gap it rejects?It drives up to where its 0.389 m keep-outs (0.10 + 0.269 + 0.02) meet the centreline and stops. The goal cost wants it forward, the slack cost (10⁴ per unit) forbids it, and the box forbids going round. That's a local minimum, the honest behaviour of a controller that believes it doesn't fit.
Where does odometry come from in Gazebo, and does it drift?From the diff-drive plugin, which integrates the simulated wheel motion. It drifts a little: 1–2 cm by the end of our runs, which is why we grade on ground truth.
Why one launch file, when Lab 1 and 2 said no launch files?Labs 1 and 2 used the course's own launch file for Gazebo. The project needs a world the course doesn't have (posts, walls, a robot with a plank, a ground-truth bridge). The launch file only does what turtlebot3_bringup does on the robot, and the controller still runs in its own terminal.
Why a 2 cm safety margin, and what did it cost?The first s1a run with three discs grazed the obstacle by 2 mm. Odometry was 2.8 cm off ground truth at that moment, and the three-disc plan left only about 4 mm of real clearance. 2 cm covers the measured error, and it's the same idea as Lab 3's inflation radius of 0.120 for a 0.105 robot. It cost 4 cm of gap: three discs now need 0.30 m instead of 0.26, so 0.28 is rejected. It still meets the proposal's 0.30 m.
What would you change for the real robot?Drop use_sim_time. Measure the actual actuation delay for delay_steps. There is no ground truth, so clearance would need a tape measure or a camera. And the plank would block nothing, since it sits above the lidar.
